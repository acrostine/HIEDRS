Grant Management ()
industry_identityHigher Education in Serbia operates under the Law on Higher Education (Zakon o visokom obrazovanju) within the European Higher Education Area. It delivers academic studies and applied studies across three cycles: bachelor, master and doctoral. The output is a graduate holding a recognised degree, accompanied by a Diploma Supplement and a transcript of ECTS credits, and research results from universities and non-university institutions of higher education. The buyers are students, who are either state-funded on budget places or self-financing, employers who hire graduates, and the state, which purchases education, research and public services through ministry funding.
industry_economicsRevenue is earned from state funding allocated for budget places fixed by enrolment quotas, tuition fees paid by self-financing students, research grants and projects including Erasmus+ and other external funding. Dominant cost lines are academic and non-academic staff salaries, facility operation, and student standard subsidies for accommodation, meals and scholarships administered through student centres. The units counted and billed are the enrolled student by funding status, the ECTS credit, and the budget place. Margin is determined by the gap between the cost of delivering a study place and the state funding or tuition per place, with quota limits constraining volume.
industry_exchangesOperators buy teaching, research and administrative inputs from staff, publishers, equipment suppliers and facility providers. They sell education to students, research results and graduates to employers, and capacity on state-funded places to the ministry responsible for higher education. The National Accreditation Body (NAT) accredits institutions and study programmes and issues Accreditation Certificates and Study Programme Accreditations as preconditions for enrolling students and issuing recognised degrees. The ministry responsible for higher education supervises institutions and allocates budget funding. The Commissioner for Information of Public Importance and Personal Data Protection oversees personal data processing under the Law on Personal Data Protection. With Erasmus+ credit mobility, institutions exchange Learning Agreements and Inter-Institutional Agreements with partner institutions, and Recognition Decisions for qualifications obtained abroad cross institutional boundaries.
industry_forcesSerbian quality assurance is read against the Standards and Guidelines for Quality Assurance in the European Higher Education Area (ESG 2015), which strengthens internal self-evaluation and external evaluation feeding NAT accreditation decisions. Erasmus+ credit mobility requires recognition of credits earned abroad using ECTS and the Diploma Supplement, pushing comparability of learning outcomes. The Serbian Law on Personal Data Protection, modelled on the EU General Data Protection Regulation, applies to student records, staff records and research participant data, with cross-border mobility and joint programmes bringing GDPR directly into play. The coexistence of integrated and federated university legal forms shifts where signing authority, accountability and records sit between university and faculty level.
industry_constraintA study programme cannot enrol students or issue recognised degrees unless it holds a valid Accreditation Certificate from the National Accreditation Body, renewed periodically, and the institution is accredited for that programme.
